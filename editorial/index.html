02 — The Craft

From mountain woolto shu.

Shu — patti in Urdu — is the handwoven woollen cloth of Chitral, made from the wool of local Kari sheep. The making is documented, unhurried, and unchanged in its essentials: washed, spun, woven, fulled by foot, dried by the sun.

  1. 01WashingThe sheep are washed in mountain streams.
  2. 02Shearing & cardingFleece is sheared, then carded and spun by hand.
  3. 03Warping & weavingWarped outdoors and woven on a wooden loom over some three weeks.
  4. 04FullingFoot-fulled — bohteek — in the hot springs of Garam Chashma.
  5. 05Sun-dryingDried in the mountain sun. No dye but walnut red.

04 — The Heritage

A valley thatmakes slowly.

Chitral sits in a high arid valley where the Hindu Kush closes in on every side, Tirich Mir above it all. Houses are stone and timber with flat mud roofs; inside, the ceiling is often a stepped diamond of timber — the gherarum — built without a single nail.

The valley’s crafts are inseparable from its animals and trees: Kari sheep for shu, walnut wood for boxes and bowls, mulberry for the long-necked sitar made in Sonoghor village, where families have made it for five or six generations and one instrument takes two to three weeks.

“Windproof and undyed-earthy: natural white, grey, black and brown, with walnut-dyed red as the one traditional accent.”

Every fact on this page comes from the documented record — museum and field accounts of Chitral’s making. Where the record is silent, we say so rather than invent.
