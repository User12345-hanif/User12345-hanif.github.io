02 — The Craft

From mountain wool to
shu.

Shu is the handwoven wool cloth of Chitral — washed in mountain streams, sheared, carded and spun by hand, warped outdoors, woven on a wooden loom over twenty to twenty-five days, then fulled by foot in the hot springs of Garam Chashma and dried in the sun.

It is a slow craft, and an endangered one. Northora begins here: with documented crafts, natural wool in its own colours — white, grey, black and brown — and walnut red as the one traditional accent. Nothing here is invented, and nothing is claimed until it is verified.

Origin
Chitral, Hindu Kush
Fibre
Kari sheep wool
Process

Handwoven Shu Cloth

Shu — patti in Urdu — is the handwoven woollen cloth of Chitral, made from the wool of local Kari sheep. The documented making runs from washing the sheep in mountain streams, through shearing, carding and spinning, to warping outdoors and weaving on a wooden loom over some three weeks, then foot-fulling in the hot springs of Garam Chashma and sun-drying. The cloth is windproof and undyed-earthy: natural white, grey, black and brown, with walnut-dyed red as the one traditional accent. This is a concept study in a length of that cloth — not a stocked item, and not a claim about any one maker's work.

Chitrali Pakol

The pakol is a soft wool cap with a flat crown and a thick rolled brim, in natural brown, black, grey or ivory wool — and in a walnut-dyed red. The brim rolls to fit and pulls down over the ears in the cold; a feather or a sprig of juniper is sometimes tucked into it. The cap's home is Gilgit–Chitral, from where it spread across the region. We make no claim for the old Alexander story about its origin: scholars do not support it. This is a concept study in the cap, not a stocked item.

Chitrali Sitar

The Chitrali sitar is a long-necked lute carved from mulberry wood, with five strings in three courses tuned cc g cc, and a middle string that passes under the first four frets. It is made in Sonoghor village in Upper Chitral, by families who have made it for five or six generations, one instrument taking two to three weeks; it is played in tea rooms to accompany Khowar singing. Its name comes from the Central Asian setar, not from the Indian sitar. This is a concept study in the instrument, not a stocked item.

Balti Pattu Cloth

Pattu — Balgos in Balti — is the documented woollen cloth of Baltistan: sheep are washed and shorn, the wool beaten clean, separated by fibre length, spun, woven and dyed with leaves and fruit. 04 — The Heritage

Made slowly, in the Hindu Kush.

Chitral’s houses are stone and timber, with flat mud roofs and a stepped-diamond timber ceiling — the gherarum — over a central hearth. The craft that grew inside them is Central Asian in its roots and Kho in its making: wool cloth, the flat-topped pakol, long woollen coats, and woodwork from the walnut trees of the valleys.

A 1968 study of the region’s weavers called shu-making an endangered craft. Northora exists to give it a patient, international audience — without inventing what it is, or who makes it.

“The mountains do not hurry. Neither do we.”
